About me

I believe technology should reduce complexity, not create it.

Most product problems aren't design problems — they're system problems: nobody sees how one decision in sales ripples into support, or how a poorly thought-out flow quietly costs the whole team adoption. That's where I step in — inside banks, SaaS platforms and fintechs — finding the exact point where complexity turns into friction, and redesigning the system, not just the screen, so it stops costing the business money.

I combine product strategy, UX, design systems and artificial intelligence with one goal: making complex systems easy for people to understand, without friction. That's why I describe myself as a Human Systems Architect: I don't just design interfaces, I design the relationship between people, technology and business.

I'm passionate about solving meaningful problems, mentoring teams, exploring the potential of AI, and building products that create opportunities for others.

Services

How I step in when your product gets stuck

Four entry points, depending on where your product is hurting today.

UX/UI & Design Systems

Your product grew by patching things together, and now every new feature takes longer and breaks something else. I audit the whole system, find where the coherence broke down, and leave a foundation your team can scale without renegotiating every decision from scratch.

AI-Assisted Prototyping

Before it reaches engineering, I validate every decision with a working prototype, not a static mockup. That means iterating in days, not sprints, and arriving at development with less rework and fewer last-minute debates.

Motion & Micro-interactions

When a flow feels slow or confusing, it's often not the logic — it's that the interface isn't telling the user what's happening. I design the feedback and transitions that make your product feel as well-built as it actually is.

Product strategy

When the roadmap fills up with features nobody can quite justify, I step in to bring order: what to build first, why, and how you'll measure whether it worked — working shoulder to shoulder with product and engineering, not from the outside.
